# Glossary

**Pixel's AI Field Guide: Missions for Curious Humans** · Version 1.0 (2026-11-06)
**Navigation:** [Start here](00-start-here.md) · [Resources](resources.md)

Every key term from the guide, in plain English, A to Z. The definitions match the **Key terms** tables of the ten topic files ([Topic 01](../../task-1/topics/01-what-is-ai.md) to [Topic 10](../../task-1/topics/10-future-of-ai.md)), so the guide, the topics and the test bank use words the same way. "First met in" links to the mission where the term is first introduced; some entries also say where it is explained in full.

**Watch out for words with two meanings:** *bias* (an unfair pattern in AI vs a number inside a neuron), *model* (a trained AI component, not a fashion model or a toy), *memory* (in the functionality lens, using the recent past while operating, not training data) and *prediction* (any best guess, not only about the future).

## A

- **Accountability:** Being clearly responsible for a decision and its consequences, and able to be held to account (for example, by a court or regulator). People and organizations, not AI systems, are accountable. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Activation function:** A simple rule that turns a neuron's weighted sum into its output, for example "output 1 if the sum is greater than 0, otherwise 0". *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Adversarial example:** An input deliberately altered, often in ways humans can't notice, to make an AI system give a wrong answer. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*
- **Agent / environment:** In reinforcement learning, the agent is the learner that acts; the environment is the world (or game) it acts in. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **AGI:** See *General AI (AGI)*. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **AI effect:** The tendency for people to stop calling something "AI" once it works well and becomes ordinary. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).*
- **AI literacy:** The knowledge and skills to understand, use and critically judge AI systems. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **AI system:** A particular piece of AI technology (for example, a spam filter or a chatbot) that receives inputs and produces outputs. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).*
- **AI winter:** A period when funding, business interest and public enthusiasm for AI fell sharply, usually after promises were not met. There were two: roughly 1974–1980 and 1987–1993. *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*
- **Algorithm:** A precise, step-by-step set of instructions a computer follows to solve a problem or complete a task. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*
- **Anthropomorphism:** Treating a non-human thing as if it had human thoughts and feelings. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*
- **Application:** A real-world use of AI to do a job for people, for example suggesting films or reading X-rays. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Artificial general intelligence (AGI):** See *General AI (AGI)*. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Artificial intelligence (AI):** Computer systems that perform tasks we would normally say need human intelligence, such as recognizing patterns, understanding language, making decisions or creating content. Also the name of the field of study that builds such systems. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).*
- **Artificial neuron:** One unit of a neural network: it multiplies each input by a weight, adds them up with a bias, and passes the result through an activation function. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Artificial superintelligence (ASI):** See *Superintelligence (ASI)*. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **ASI:** See *Superintelligence (ASI)*. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Autonomous vehicle:** A vehicle that can drive itself without a human driver in some or all situations. A robotaxi is an autonomous vehicle used as a taxi. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*

## B

- **Backpropagation:** A training method that tells every weight in a multi-layer neural network how to change to reduce the error. Popularized in 1986, it made deep networks trainable. *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*
- **Benchmark / dataset:** A shared collection of examples (for example, labeled images) used to train and compare AI systems. ImageNet is a famous one. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).* Benchmarks in history: [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).
- **Bias (in a neuron):** An extra number added to a neuron's weighted sum that shifts how easily it "fires". Not the same as *unfair* bias. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Bias (in AI):** When an AI system's outputs are systematically less accurate or less fair for some groups of people than for others. It can enter at every stage of a system's life cycle. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Black box:** A system whose internal workings are too complex for people to inspect directly. Large neural networks are often described this way. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Breadth-first search (BFS):** A search method that checks every state reachable in 1 move, then every state reachable in 2 moves, and so on, using a frontier (first in, first out) and a visited set. The first solution it finds uses the fewest moves. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*
- **Brittleness:** When a system works well on familiar inputs but fails, often badly and confidently, on inputs that differ slightly from its training data. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).* Expert systems were already described as brittle in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md) and [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).

## C

- **Capability lens (Lens 1):** Classifying AI by how broad and powerful its abilities are compared with humans: narrow (ANI), general (AGI), superintelligence (ASI). *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Classification / regression:** Two kinds of supervised task. Classification predicts a category (spam / not spam). Regression predicts a number (a house price, a bus arrival time). *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Classification scheme (taxonomy):** A way of sorting things into groups according to one chosen question. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Clustering:** An unsupervised task: grouping similar examples together without being told the groups. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Cold-start problem:** A recommender that relies on ratings cannot recommend a new item nobody has rated (or serve a new user with no history), because there is nothing to compare. Using the item's features helps. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Collaborative filtering:** A recommendation method based on the idea "people who liked similar things in the past will like similar things in future". *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Combinatorial explosion:** The problem that the number of possibilities to check grows enormously as a problem gets bigger, so "try every option" methods become impossibly slow. *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*
- **Computer vision:** AI that interprets images or video, for example finding pedestrians in a camera feed. Today it is mostly done with deep learning. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Constraints (in search):** The rules that say which states are allowed, for example "never leave the wolf with the goat without the farmer". *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*
- **Context window:** The maximum amount of text (measured in tokens) a model can take into account at once. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*

## D

- **Data:** Recorded information a computer can process: text, numbers, images, sounds, clicks, sensor readings. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*
- **Data minimization:** Collecting and keeping only the personal data that is really needed for a purpose. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Deep learning:** Machine learning using neural networks with many layers (in particular, more than one hidden layer). "Deep" means many layers, not deep understanding. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).* Explained in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).
- **Deepfake:** Realistic fake audio, image or video of a real person, made with generative AI. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Deployer:** See *Provider / deployer*. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Digital Omnibus on AI:** An EU law that amended the EU AI Act in 2026, mainly delaying the high-risk rules (to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028) and adding a new prohibition. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Disaggregated evaluation:** Checking a system's results separately for each group of people (for example, by skin type and gender) instead of only overall, so that gaps between groups are not hidden. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Discriminative (predictive) model:** A model that sorts or scores an input rather than creating new content, for example spam / not spam. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Distribution shift:** When the data a model meets in use differ from the data it was trained on (for example, night photos for a model trained on day photos). *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).* Explained in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).

## E

- **ELIZA:** A rule-based chatbot described by Joseph Weizenbaum in 1966. Its DOCTOR script imitated a therapist by matching keywords and reflecting the user's words back, with no understanding. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).* Investigated in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).
- **ELIZA effect:** People's tendency to believe a computer understands them because it produces human-like responses. *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*
- **Epoch:** One complete pass through the training data during training. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Ethics:** Thinking carefully about what is right and fair, and about who is helped or harmed by a choice. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **EU AI Act:** The European Union's law on artificial intelligence, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in force since 1 August 2024. It sorts AI uses by risk and sets rules for each level, applying in stages. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Expert system:** A rule-based program that stores the knowledge of human experts as many IF-THEN rules, for example to configure computers or suggest diagnoses. *First met in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md).* The 1980s expert-systems boom: [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).
- **Explainability:** The ability to give a human-understandable reason for a specific AI output ("why was my loan refused?"). *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*

## F

- **Fact (in Prolog):** A statement the program simply treats as true, for example `parent(grace, ada).` *First met in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md).*
- **False positive / false negative:** A false positive is a wrong "yes" or false alarm (a genuine email sent to spam). A false negative is a wrong "no" or missed case (a spam email that reaches the inbox). *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).* Explained in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).
- **Feature:** A measurable property of an input that the model uses, for example "the email contains the word *free*". *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Filter bubble:** When a recommender keeps showing you more of what you already saw, so your view narrows over time. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Fine-tuning:** Further training of a pre-trained model on a smaller, carefully chosen set of examples to make it better at a particular job, such as following instructions. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Forecast:** A prediction about the future, often with a probability ("50% chance by year X"). *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Four-step check:** A routine for checking AI output that matters: split it into claims, prioritize the risky ones, verify them in an independent reliable source, then decide (keep, correct, remove or label as unverified). *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*
- **Functionality lens (Lens 2):** Classifying AI by how it uses memory and whether it understands others or itself: reactive machines, limited memory, theory of mind, self-aware. A popular-science taxonomy (Hintze, 2016), not a formal standard. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*

## G

- **General AI (AGI):** Hypothetical AI that could match (or exceed) human abilities across essentially any intellectual task, and learn new tasks as flexibly as a person. There is no single agreed definition; the mainstream view is that current LLMs are not AGI (debated). Sometimes called "strong AI". *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).* The AGI debate: [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).
- **Generalization:** A model's ability to work well on new data, not just on its training data. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **General-purpose AI (GPAI) model:** A model, such as a large LLM, that can be used for many different tasks and built into many products. The EU AI Act has separate rules for these. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Generative AI:** AI that creates new content, such as text, images, audio, video or computer code, usually in response to a prompt. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).* Explained in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).
- **Governance:** All the ways society steers a technology: laws, standards, company policies, international agreements, oversight bodies. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **GPU (graphics processing unit):** A computer chip originally designed for video-game graphics that turned out to be very good at the many simple calculations neural networks need. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).* Its role in AI history: [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).
- **Grounding:** Connecting an AI's answer to a reliable source of facts (for example, a document or search result) instead of relying only on patterns learned in training. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*

## H

- **Hallucination:** When a generative AI system, especially an LLM, produces content that sounds confident and fluent but is false or made up, such as a fake quotation or a non-existent reference. It is not lying: the model has no intention. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).* Explained in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).
- **Human oversight:** People monitoring, checking or being able to override an AI system's outputs. "Human-in-the-loop" means a person must approve each decision. The higher the stakes, the more oversight is needed. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Hype cycle:** The repeating pattern of excitement, inflated promises, disappointment and (sometimes) steady recovery that new technologies often go through. *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*
- **Hypothetical / speculative:** Imagined and discussed, but not built. "Speculative" suggests even more uncertainty about whether it is possible. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*

## I

- **Imitation game:** See *Turing test*. *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*
- **Inference (prediction):** Using a trained model on a new input to produce an output. The model's parameters stay fixed during inference. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Input:** The information an AI system receives: text you type, a photo, a sound recording, sensor readings, your clicks. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).*

## J

- **Job displacement:** Existing jobs disappearing or changing so much that the workers need to move to other work. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*

## K

- **Knowledge cutoff:** The date after which a model has no training data, so it doesn't "know" later events unless given them. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*

## L

- **Label:** The correct answer attached to a training example, for example "spam" or "not spam". *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Language model:** A model that gives probabilities for what text comes next. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Large language model (LLM):** A very large deep-learning language model, trained on enormous amounts of text, that generates text by repeatedly predicting the next token. Most are transformers. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Layer (input, hidden, output):** A group of neurons at the same stage. The input layer receives the features; hidden layers are in between; the output layer gives the result. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Learning rate:** A training setting that controls how big each adjustment to the weights is. Too big: training is unstable; too small: it is painfully slow. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Limitation:** A real weakness of current AI, for example inventing facts or failing on unusual inputs. A problem to manage. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*
- **Limited memory AI:** AI that uses information from the recent past (for example, the last few seconds of sensor data, or the conversation so far) while it is working, but does not build up lasting life experience. Training data does not count as "memory" here. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Loss (training loss, test loss):** A number measuring a model's average error, on the training data (training loss) or on the test data (test loss). Lower is better. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*

## M

- **Machine learning (ML):** AI in which a learning algorithm finds patterns in example data and produces a model, instead of a person writing all the rules. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).* Explained in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).
- **Markov-chain text generator:** An early, non-neural way to generate text: each next word is chosen at random according to which words followed the current word in example text (demonstrated by Claude Shannon in 1948). *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Medical imaging:** Pictures of the inside of the body, such as X-rays, CT scans, MRI scans and photos of the retina (back of the eye). *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Misconception:** A widespread false belief about AI, for example "AI is conscious" or "AI is always objective". A problem to correct. *First met in [Mission 11](mission-11-myth-busters.md).*
- **Misinformation / disinformation:** False information. Misinformation is spread by mistake; disinformation is spread deliberately to deceive. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Model:** The part of an AI system that turns an input into an output. In machine learning, the model is produced by training on data and stores the patterns that were learned. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).* Explained in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).

## N

- **Narrow AI (ANI):** AI built for one task or a limited range of tasks. All AI that exists today is narrow AI. Sometimes called "weak AI". *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Neural network:** A model made of many simple connected units (artificial neurons) arranged in layers, loosely inspired by brain cells. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).*
- **Next-token prediction:** How an LLM generates text: given all the text so far, it works out a probability for every possible next token, picks one (usually by sampling, so not always the single most likely), adds it to the text and repeats. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*

## O

- **OECD AI Principles:** Non-binding principles on trustworthy AI adopted by the OECD in 2019 and updated in May 2024: five values-based principles and five recommendations for governments, with 47 adherents. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Output:** What an AI system produces: a prediction, a classification, a recommendation, a decision, or new content. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).*
- **Overfitting:** When a model fits its training data so closely (even memorizing it) that it does badly on new data. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*

## P

- **Parameters (weights):** The adjustable numbers inside a model that are set during training. They control how much each feature counts. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Perceptron:** An early, single-layer artificial neural network (Rosenblatt, 1958) that could learn simple yes/no classifications from examples. *First met in [Mission 05](mission-05-inside-the-neuron.md).* Its place in history: [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).
- **Personal data:** Information that identifies a person or can be linked to them: name, face, location, voice, browsing history. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Personalization:** Changing what each user sees based on data about that user. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Prediction:** A best guess about something unknown, for example "this email is probably spam" or "traffic will be heavy at 5 pm". In AI, a prediction does not have to be about the future. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).*
- **Pre-training:** The first, largest stage of LLM training: learning to predict the next token on a huge collection of text. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Privacy:** People's control over information about themselves: what is collected, how it is used, and who sees it. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Probability:** A number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain) saying how likely something is; 0.41 means 41%. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Prolog:** A logic programming language: you write facts and rules, then ask queries, and Prolog works out the answers that follow logically from what you wrote. *First met in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md).*
- **Prompt:** The text (or image, or other input) a person gives a generative AI system to tell it what to produce. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Provider / deployer:** Under the EU AI Act, the provider develops an AI system and puts it on the market; the deployer is an organization that uses it (for example, an employer using a hiring tool). *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Proxy:** A measurable stand-in for something hard to measure, for example using "past healthcare spending" as a stand-in for "how sick someone is". A poor proxy can create bias. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*

## Q

- **Query (in Prolog):** A question you ask a Prolog program, for example `parent(ada, Child).`; Prolog answers from its facts and rules. *First met in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md).*

## R

- **Reactive machine:** AI that responds only to the current input, with no memory of earlier moments. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Recommendation system (recommender):** AI that predicts which items (films, songs, products, posts) a person is likely to want, and shows those first. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Regulation:** Laws and rules made by governments that organizations must follow, with penalties if they don't. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Reinforcement learning:** Machine learning by trial and error: an agent takes actions in an environment and receives rewards or penalties. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).* Previewed in the [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md) maze.
- **Risk tiers (EU AI Act):** The EU AI Act's four levels: unacceptable risk (banned), high risk (allowed with strict obligations), transparency risk (disclosure duties, often called "limited risk") and minimal risk (no specific new obligations). *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Risk-based approach:** Regulating more strictly where the potential harm is greater, and lightly or not at all where it is small. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **RLHF:** Reinforcement learning from human feedback: people compare or rank the model's answers, and the model is trained to produce answers people prefer. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Rule (in Prolog):** A statement that something is true IF other things are true, for example `grandparent(X, Z) :- parent(X, Y), parent(Y, Z).` *First met in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md).*
- **Rule-based AI (symbolic AI):** AI in which people write the knowledge down as explicit rules and symbols (for example "IF ... THEN ..."), and the computer reasons or searches with them. Sometimes called "traditional" AI or GOFAI ("good old-fashioned AI"). *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).* Built in [Mission 03](mission-03-pixels-rulebook.md).

## S

- **Sampling:** Choosing the next token at random, but in proportion to the probabilities, so likely tokens are chosen more often. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Search (in AI):** Solving a problem by systematically exploring possible states and actions until a goal is found. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*
- **Self-aware AI:** Hypothetical AI that would have a sense of itself and awareness of its own internal states (consciousness). *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Sensitivity:** Of all the people who really have a condition, the share that a test correctly flags. Low sensitivity means missed cases. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Six-part framework:** This guide's way of analyzing any AI application: input, output, AI type, benefit, risk, human oversight. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Soft law:** Non-binding principles and recommendations (for example, OECD, UNESCO) that guide governments but cannot directly fine anyone. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Specificity:** Of all the people who really do not have a condition, the share that a test correctly clears. Low specificity means false alarms. *First met in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).*
- **Stakeholder:** Anyone affected by a system: users, the people it makes decisions about, the company, workers, society. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Stakes:** How serious the consequences are if the AI gets it wrong. *First met in [Mission 01](mission-01-is-it-ai.md).* Explained in [Mission 09](mission-09-ai-in-the-wild.md).
- **State / state space:** A state is a snapshot of a situation at one moment (for example, who is on which side of a river). The state space is all the possible states and the moves between them. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*
- **Superintelligence (ASI):** Speculative AI that would greatly exceed the best human abilities in virtually every area. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Supervised learning:** Machine learning from examples that come with the correct answers (labels). *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Symbolic AI:** See *Rule-based AI (symbolic AI)*. *First met in [Mission 02](mission-02-the-river-problem.md).*

## T

- **Temperature:** A setting that controls how much randomness sampling uses: low temperature means more predictable, high temperature means more varied. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Theory of mind AI:** Hypothetical AI that would understand that people (and other agents) have their own thoughts, feelings, beliefs and intentions, and adjust its behavior accordingly. *First met in [Mission 07](mission-07-sorting-the-robots.md).*
- **Threshold:** A cut-off value: if a score is at or above it, the model gives one answer; below it, the other answer. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Token:** The unit of text an LLM works with: a whole word, part of a word, a punctuation mark or a single character. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).*
- **Training:** The process of running a learning algorithm on example data so that it produces (or improves) a model. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Training data:** The examples a model learns from. If they are unbalanced or reflect past unfairness, the model can learn that pattern. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Training set / test set:** The training set is the data the model learns from. The test set is labeled data kept aside and used only to check how well the model works on examples it has never seen. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Transfer learning:** Reusing a model already trained on a big dataset and retraining only a small part of it (for example, its last layer) for a new task. Teachable Machine works this way. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **Transformer:** A neural-network design, introduced in 2017, that uses "attention" to process whole sequences of tokens at once. It is the basis of today's large language models. *First met in [Mission 06](mission-06-the-word-guessing-machine.md).* Its place in history: [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).
- **Transparency:** Being open about when AI is used, what data it uses, and how it reaches its outputs. *First met in [Mission 10](mission-10-the-fairness-files.md).*
- **Turing test (imitation game):** A test proposed by Alan Turing in 1950: if a judge chatting by text cannot reliably tell a machine from a human, should we say the machine "thinks"? *First met in [Mission 08](mission-08-time-machine.md).*

## U

- **Underfitting:** When a model is too simple to capture the pattern, so it does poorly even on its training data. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
- **UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI:** A non-binding global standard on AI ethics adopted by all 193 UNESCO member states in November 2021, built on four core values and ten principles. *First met in [Mission 12](mission-12-tomorrows-rulebook.md).*
- **Unsupervised learning:** Machine learning from examples without labels; the algorithm looks for structure such as groups of similar items. *First met in [Mission 04](mission-04-learning-from-examples.md).*
