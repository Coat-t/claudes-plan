# Resources: Videos, Platforms and Further Reading

**Pixel's AI Field Guide: Missions for Curious Humans** · Version 1.0 (2026-11-06)
**Navigation:** [Start here](00-start-here.md) · [Glossary](glossary.md)

This page collects every external resource the guide uses, with a short introduction to each: what it is, why it is here, which part matters, what to do with it, what to watch out for, how to access it, and what to do instead if you can't. **You never need these resources to complete a mission**: every mission contains its own explanations, data and a fallback for each platform. The resources make ideas more vivid, let you experiment, or take you further.

**Credit.** All videos, platforms and readings on this page are **external resources** made and owned by their creators (named in each entry). They are linked, not copied. Pixel, Dr. Ada Okafor, the Curiosity Lab, the diagrams and the supplements in [`../activities/`](../activities/) were created for this guide.

**When things were checked.** All links on this page were checked on **6 November 2026**. Video titles, creators, durations, caption tracks and timestamps were read from each video's public YouTube data; every timestamp comes from YouTube's own chapter list for that video. Platform controls and settings were checked against each platform's live page, source code or official help. See the **verification status** in each platform entry for what was, and was not, tested hands-on. **Teachers:** please watch each recommended segment once before using it with a class, and click through each platform on your learners' devices.

### B1. Google Teachable Machine

- **What:** a free browser tool by Google for training small image, sound or pose classifiers. <https://teachablemachine.withgoogle.com/> (version 2.4.18 observed on 2026-11-06).
- **Why:** it makes supervised learning visible: you collect labeled examples, train, test on new inputs and see how the training data shapes the results.
- **Used in:** Mission 04, Activity 4.3 (train, test, find mistakes, fix with varied data) and Mission 10, Activity 10.2 (unbalanced data and testing by person).
- **Access:** no account or login. A Google account is only needed for the optional "Save to Drive". Designed for a laptop or desktop browser; the app recommends Chrome or Firefox; phones and tablets were not tested. Webcam permission is needed for webcam capture; without a webcam, upload .png or .jpg photos (cropped to squares).
- **Privacy:** according to the official FAQ, your samples stay in your browser tab and the model trains there, unless you save to Google Drive or upload your model. **"Upload my model" publishes the model to Google's servers, where anyone with the link can use it: don't use it for these activities.** Nothing is saved when you close the tab.
- **Tested settings:** Image Project -> Standard image model; three classes (`thumbs up`, `thumbs down`, `nothing`); default Advanced settings (Epochs 50, Batch Size 16, Learning Rate 0.001); about 40–60 webcam samples per class. "Under the hood" holds back 15% of samples for testing. Keep the tab open while training.
- **Watch out for:** a tiny transfer-learning demo; results vary with camera, lighting and person; confidence bars are not calibrated probabilities.
- **Verification status:** *verified* by page fetch and inspection of the app's source and text files: the URL, version, no-account and data-stays-in-browser statements, button labels, default settings and the tab-switch warning. *Not verified hands-on:* the full click-through, webcam permission on school devices, training time, actual confidence values, phone and tablet behavior.
- **Fallback:** both missions include an illustrative (made-up) results table with the same analysis questions.

### B2. TensorFlow Playground

- **What:** "A Neural Network Playground", an open-source visualizer by the TensorFlow team. <https://playground.tensorflow.org/>
- **Why:** you can watch a small neural network learn on 2-D data and change one thing at a time (layers, neurons, learning rate, features).
- **Used in:** Mission 05 (preset links P1–P10 for: no hidden layer vs one hidden layer; too few neurons; learning rate too high or too low; spiral data; overfitting).
- **Access:** no account, no install, no webcam. Desktop or laptop recommended (wide layout; phones not tested).
- **Tested settings:** each preset link sets everything through the web address (for example `networkShape=4`, `learningRate=0.03`, `dataset=circle`). The live page's options match the open-source code (last changed in 2022).
- **Watch out for:** **results vary from run to run** because each run starts with random weights and, unless fixed, randomly generated data; the patterns in Mission 05 are typical, not guaranteed, and a run occasionally gets stuck (press Reset). Toy data (500 points in 2-D), not real images or text.
- **Verification status:** *verified:* URL, controls and options, the meaning of every preset-link setting (from the source code), and the expected behaviors, via an independent re-implementation of the Playground's training algorithm run with several random seeds. *Not verified hands-on:* that each preset opens correctly in a browser, exact numbers and speed, phone layout.
- **Fallback:** Mission 05 includes the simulated results table, labeled as simulation output.

### B3. SWISH (SWI-Prolog for SHaring)

- **What:** a free, browser-based Prolog environment from the SWI-Prolog project. <https://swish.swi-prolog.org/> (help: <https://swish.swi-prolog.org/help/help.html>)
- **Why:** you can write facts and rules and ask queries, seeing a rule-based system reason step by step.
- **Used in:** Mission 03 and the [Prolog supplement](../activities/prolog/).
- **Access:** works **signed out**; logging in (Google or StackOverflow) is optional and only for saving. Program goes in the left editor, the query in the bottom-right box; each run starts fresh. Laptop or desktop recommended (mobile not tested).
- **Privacy:** you don't need to save. **If you save, programs are public and searchable by default**; untick "Public" and never put personal information in code.
- **Tested settings:** every query in the Prolog supplement was run automatically with SWI-Prolog (the system behind SWISH) and cross-checked with a second Prolog engine (results in the supplement's `test-output.txt`); the small "Pixel's friends" knowledge base used in Mission 03 was run on the live SWISH server.
- **Watch out for:** a shared public server that may be busy; "false" means "cannot be proved from the facts given", not "known to be untrue".
- **Verification status:** *verified:* URL, signed-out use and saving behavior (official help), the preload-link feature, and query results (run on the live server). *Not verified:* the exact wording of the start-screen buttons, and mobile use.
- **Fallback:** install SWI-Prolog (<https://www.swi-prolog.org>) to work offline, or use the recorded results tables with the predict-then-check questions.

### B4. ELIZA, 1966-faithful reconstruction

- **What:** a reconstruction of Joseph Weizenbaum's 1966 ELIZA running the original DOCTOR script, by Anthony Hay and Max Hay, version 1.00 (2025-03-08). <https://anthay.github.io/eliza.html>. Its authors state that its replies should match what the 1966 program would have given; the script is a transcription of the one published in the 1966 paper. Implementation released as CC0; DOCTOR script (c) 1966 ACM.
- **Why:** you can probe a rule-based chatbot, find its keywords and patterns, and see why fluent replies are not understanding.
- **Used in:** Mission 08, Activity 8.2.
- **Access:** no account, no install; any modern browser. One self-contained file, so you can save it and use it offline.
- **Tested settings:** commands start with a star: `*traceauto` (show which keyword and pattern fired after each reply), `*key` (list keywords), `*reset`, `*cacm` (replay the 1966 published conversation; press Enter to step, Escape to stop), `*help`.
- **Watch out for:** replies are in capitals and cycle through fixed lists; the DOCTOR script imitates a therapist, so use the guide's made-up test sentences, not real personal problems.
- **Verification status:** *verified:* page live; version; the commands above (read from the page's code); the DOCTOR script rules, from which the guide's expected replies were worked out. *Not verified hands-on:* typing the inputs in a browser; if your replies differ, record the difference.
- **Alternative:** Norbert Landsteiner's *elizabot* (2005), <https://www.masswerk.at/elizabot/eliza.html>, a DOCTOR-like version with added phrases that **picks replies at random**, so answers vary between learners.
- **Fallback:** Mission 08 prints the start of Weizenbaum's 1966 published conversation, with analysis questions and answers.
- **Not usable:** an older ELIZA page often linked in courses (web.njit.edu/~ronkowit/eliza.html) returned "page not found" on 6 November 2026.

## C. Further reading

1. **Elements of AI** (MinnaLearn and the University of Helsinki). <https://www.elementsofai.com/>. A free online introduction to AI for non-experts, with no programming or complicated maths. **Use it** as a self-paced next step after this guide. *Access:* exercises and progress appear to need a free course account; reading without an account was not checked.
2. **IBM, "What Is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?"** (Cole Stryker and Eda Kavlakoglu; updated 2026). <https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/artificial-intelligence>. A plain reference page on AI, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI, with a "Weak AI vs. Strong AI" section (the capability lens from Mission 07). *Watch out:* a company page, with a commercial angle.
3. **3Blue1Brown written lessons:** <https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/neural-networks> and <https://www.3blue1brown.com/lessons/mini-llm>. Text versions of V4 and V6, for readers or anyone who can't use YouTube.
4. **Code.org "How AI Works"** hub: <https://code.org/ai/how-ai-works> (redirects to Code.org's curriculum page). The full short-video series behind V2, V3, V7, V10 and V12, with lesson plans.
5. **The topic files behind this guide:** [Topic 01](../../task-1/topics/01-what-is-ai.md) to [Topic 10](../../task-1/topics/10-future-of-ai.md). Each has fuller explanations, a worked example, extra activities with answers, and a numbered list of the sources used.
6. **For Mission 12's rules:** the European Commission's AI Act page, <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai>; the OECD AI Principles, <https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles>; and UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, <https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics>. Official pages; laws and dates change, so these are where to check the current version.

- **Quick, Draw!** (Google): a fun drawing game in which a neural network guesses your doodle. Left out because its page says your drawings are added to a public research dataset; learners should not contribute data without understanding and agreeing to that. Teachers who want it can use its data browser, <https://quickdraw.withgoogle.com/data>, without drawing.
- **Longer backup videos** (*Supervised Learning: Crash Course AI #2*, 15:23; *Machine Learning & Artificial Intelligence: Crash Course Computer Science #34*, 11:51; *How Computer Vision Works*, Code.org, 6:24): accurate but longer or more technical than the chosen ones.
- **A "types of AI" video** for Mission 07: the short videos found mix the capability and functionality lenses into one list, the very confusion that mission fixes.
- **Australia's National AI Centre pages:** could not be loaded for checking on 6 November 2026, so they are not recommended here.

## E. If a link breaks

Websites move. If a link on this page stops working:

1. **Use the mission's fallback.** Every [TRY IT] and [WATCH] box says what to do without the resource; you lose nothing essential.
2. **Use the listed alternative** (TED.com transcript, Code.org's own video copy, 3Blue1Brown's written lesson, the masswerk ELIZA, offline SWI-Prolog).
3. **Search for the exact title and creator** given in the entry, on the creator's own site or channel. Titles and creators are listed exactly as they appeared on 6 November 2026.
4. **Try an archived copy:** paste the address into the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, <https://web.archive.org/>. (Archived copies of interactive platforms may not work.)
5. **Tell your teacher or the guide's maintainers** which link broke, so it can be replaced in the next version.

Timestamps can also shift if a creator re-edits a video. If a chapter title no longer matches, use the chapter names given in the entry to find the right part.
