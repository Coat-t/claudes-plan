Mission: Pixel in the Maze

Pixel's AI Field Guide · Curiosity Lab supplement · Version 1.0 (2026-11-06)

[MISSION BRIEF] Dr. Ada Okafor has built two practice mazes. Pixel starts at S and must reach the charging station G. Pixel can move one square north, east, south or west, and cannot walk through walls. You will watch Pixel try three very different strategies and compare them in the table at the bottom.

  1. Fixed rule (right-hand wall follower): no map, no memory, no learning. Just one rule, repeated.
  2. Search (breadth-first search, BFS): Pixel is given the whole map and plans a route before moving.
  3. Reinforcement learning (tabular Q-learning): Pixel has no map. It learns from rewards by trial and error over many attempts.

Every button works with the keyboard (Tab to move, Enter or Space to press). Pixel is a fictional robot; the three algorithms are real.

A. Fixed rule: right-hand wall follower

Pixel's only rule: "If I can turn right, turn right. Otherwise go straight. Otherwise turn left. Otherwise turn back." Pixel starts facing east and only senses the four squares next to it.

[PREDICT] Will this rule always reach G? Will it take the shortest route?

B. Search: breadth-first search (BFS)

Pixel is handed the full map. It explores outward from S, one step further each round (all squares 1 move away, then all squares 2 moves away, and so on), until it reaches G. Then it follows the route it found.

[PREDICT] How many squares will Pixel explore before finding G? Will the route be the shortest?

C. Reinforcement learning: tabular Q-learning

Pixel gets no map. In each episode (attempt), it starts at S and moves until it reaches G. It receives a reward after every move: -1 per move, -5 for bumping into a wall (Pixel stays put), +10 for reaching G. Pixel keeps a table with a score (a "Q-value") for every square and every move, and updates it after each move. Usually it picks the move with the best score. In 20% of moves it tries a random move instead, to explore.

[PREDICT] How many moves will the first episode take? After how many episodes will Pixel's best route be the shortest one?

[DEBRIEF] Expected results (open after you have tried)
  • Maze 1: the wall follower reaches G, but only after walking to the end of the long dead-end corridor on the left and back: 28 moves, while the shortest route is 16 moves. BFS explores 23 squares and finds the 16-move route. Q-learning's first episodes are long and look random (117 moves in episode 1). After 10 episodes Pixel still has no complete route. From about episode 20 onwards, its best route is the shortest one, 16 moves, the same length as BFS's route.
  • Maze 2: G sits inside a walled "island" whose walls do not touch the outer wall. The wall follower keeps its right hand on the outer wall and goes round and round; the page stops it when Pixel returns to the same square facing the same way. With the built-in settings it is stopped after 25 moves. BFS explores 27 squares and finds the 12-move route. Q-learning learns a 12-move route too (from about episode 22 onwards), because it does not follow walls: it learns which move pays off in each square.
  • Key idea: the fixed rule is cheap and needs no map, but it only works in some mazes. Search needs a full map and guarantees the shortest route. Reinforcement learning needs no map but many trial runs, and what it learns fits only this maze: move G and Pixel must learn again.

[LAB NOTE] This is a toy world: a tiny grid, perfect sensors, moves that always work, and the same maze in every episode. Real robots face noisy sensors and slipping wheels, and real reinforcement-learning systems use far more trials and often neural networks instead of a table (deep reinforcement learning). The reward numbers here (-1, -5, +10) were chosen by a human; different rewards can lead to different behavior.
